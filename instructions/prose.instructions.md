# Prose Style

These rules apply to prose in documentation, text files, comments, and docstrings.

- Comments end with a period. Place comments on separate lines before code and explain why, not
  what. Avoid appending comments to code lines. Short TODO or note comments on `from __future__`
  imports or similar one-off markers may remain inline.
- Do not use em-dashes. Rewrite with a comma, period, or separate sentence.
- Do not use semicolons as sentence punctuation. Use a comma or period.
- Put commas and periods outside closing quotation marks. Write "SEPT", not "SEPT,".
- Put punctuation outside backticks. Never end inline code with a comma or period.
- Use one space between sentences.
- Use simple, direct wording. Name the component, event, condition, or behavior. Avoid filler words
  such as "generally", "basically", "roughly", "seems", and "typically" unless uncertainty
  matters.
- Prefer concrete, testable statements over broad claims.
- Avoid unnecessary hyphenated compound modifiers when the meaning is clear. Preserve established
  terms such as P-state.
- Prefer avoiding possessive `'s` for inanimate subjects when a rephrase reads well. Possessives are
  fine for people, organizations, and named roles.
- Prefer plural subjects for general statements.
- Use established project terminology. Do not invent new terms unless needed for clarity.
- Keep each paragraph focused on one idea and to about seven lines of at most 100 characters.
  Bridge adjacent paragraphs unless they already discuss closely related things.
